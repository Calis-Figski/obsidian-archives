About FCP

Federal Containment and Protection is a civilian public-safety agency. We exist to keep dangerous materials and sites from harming people, property, and the environment. Our staff include engineers, chemists, health physicists, emergency planners, and field technicians who work alongside fire departments, hospitals, and local governments.

Our standards are reviewed every year by independent safety auditors, and our incident summaries are shared with partner agencies so lessons learned reach every region.

How We Contain Hazards

1

Identify

Reports from the public, inspectors, and partner agencies are logged and verified by a regional duty officer.

2

Assess

Specialists measure the hazard, map who could be exposed, and assign a risk rating.

3

Isolate

Crews set up barriers, ventilation controls, or sealed storage so the hazard cannot spread.

4

Monitor and Resolve

Sensors and scheduled inspections continue until the hazard is neutralized, removed, or permanently secured.

Hazard Categories

Chemical

Toxic, corrosive, flammable, and reactive substances in industrial or transport settings.

Biological

Disease-causing organisms and contaminated materials from labs, hospitals, and farms.

Radiological

Medical, industrial, and research sources that give off radiation and need shielded storage.

Structural and Industrial

Failing tanks, unstable sites, and abandoned facilities that put nearby communities at risk.

Public Safety Tips

  • Store household chemicals in their original labeled containers, away from heat and children.
  • Never mix cleaning products, especially bleach with ammonia or acids.
  • Know your local evacuation routes and keep a go-bag ready.
  • If you smell gas or see an unknown leak, leave the area first and report it from a safe distance.
  • Follow instructions from local emergency officials during any incident.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who does FCP work with?

We partner with local fire and police departments, public health offices, environmental agencies, and private industry to coordinate responses.

Is it safe to live near a containment facility?

Yes. Facilities meet strict federal standards, are inspected on a regular schedule, and have emergency plans shared with nearby communities.

How do I report a hazard?

If there is immediate danger, call your local emergency number. For non-urgent concerns, use the hazard reporting line below.

Does FCP offer training?

Yes. We run workshops for businesses, schools, and community groups on safe storage, spill response, and evacuation.
